# Stability analysis of consistent splitting implicit-explicit multistep methods up to ninth-order accuracy for incompressible flows 

Yuanyuan Kang ∗ Hong-lin Liao † Guidong Liu ‡

August 25, 2026 

Abstract 

This work presents a concise, unified stability theory of high-order decoupled implicit-explicit linear multistep (IELM) methods based on the well-known consistent splitting technique for the incompressible Navier-Stokes equation (INSE). With the help of the recent semi-generating function approach and the global discrete energy analysis, one can establish the unconditional stability of a consistent splitting IELM method with respect to the ℓ∞(H1) ∩ ℓ2(H2) norm if the associated implicit-explicit controllability intensity is larger than √2/2, a constant determined by the Stokes pressure estimate. It is shown that the β-parameterized GBDF-k (2 ≤ k ≤ 5) schemes and γ-parameterized SIELM-k (2 ≤ k ≤ 9) schemes can fulfill this requirement of implicit-explicit controllability intensity by choosing proper parameters so that they can theoretically maintain the unconditional stability of the associated consistent splitting IELM methods. Numerical experiments are also included to support our theory. 

Keywords: incompressible Navier-Stokes, implicit-explicit multistep methods, semi-generating function approach, implicit-explicit controllability intensity, unconditional stability

As an efficient decoupled approach that does not suffer from the splitting error and can achieve full-order accuracy in strong norms, the so-called gauge method [8,21] or well-known consistent splitting technique [9,14,19,20,22] computes the pressure p with a weak form Poisson equation by testing the momentum equation (1.1) against gradients. That is, by taking the L2 inner product of (1.1) with ∇ϕ and using the incompressible condition (1.2), we find that 

⟨∇ p, ∇ϕ⟩ = ⟨f + ν∆u − u · ∇ u, ∇ϕ⟩ for ∀ϕ ∈ H1(Ω) . (1.3) This weak form Poisson equation can split the computation of the velocity u and the pressure p of INSE model (1.1)-(1.2) into two consecutive steps. Once the velocity u is known, one can compute the current pressure p via the Poisson equation (1.3) and update the velocity from the momentum equation (1.1) with the no-slip boundary condition by using the previous values of the pressure. In this way, one can construct fully decoupled time approximations of INSE model (1.1)-(1.2) by the following unconstrained INSE system 

⟨∇ p, ∇ϕ⟩ = ⟨ν∆u − ν∇∇ · u − g, ∇ϕ⟩ for ∀ϕ ∈ H1(Ω) , (1.4) 

∂tu = ν∆u − ∇ p − g for x ∈ Ω with u = 0 on ∂Ω, (1.5) subjected to the initial data u = u0 at t = 0 , where the notation g := u · ∇ u − f . Here the grad-div term ν∇∇ · u in the pressure Poisson equation (1.4) is added to improve the stability of numerical approximations [14, 19, 20]. As proved in [19, Theorem 5.1], the divergence w := ∇ · u in the unconstrained INSE system (1.4)-(1.5) is a classical solution of the heat equation ∂tw = ν∆w with no-flux boundary conditions, n · ∇ w = 0 on ∂Ω. This property is practically useful in developing stable numerical algorithms since any residues of the numerical divergence would be over controlled especially near the no-slip boundary. Alternatively, due to the identity ∆u = ∇∇ · u − ∇ × ∇ × u,the modification in (1.4) can be also viewed as the replacement of ∆u by −∇ × ∇ × u to improve the accuracy of fractional-step splitting procedures [9, 22]. Consider the time mesh 0 = t0 < t 1 < · · · < t N = T with the time-step size τ = tj − tj−1 for 

j ≥ 1, and denote the difference quotient ∂τ vj := ( vj − vj−1)/τ for any mesh function vj . Let uk

and pk be the numerical approximation of Uk := u(x, t k) and P k := p(x, t k), respectively, at the discrete time level tk for 0 ≤ k ≤ N . The classical first-order consistent splitting scheme [19, 20] 

⟨∇ pn, ∇ϕ⟩ = ⟨ν∆un − ν∇∇ · un − gn, ∇ϕ⟩ for ∀ϕ ∈ H1(Ω) and n ≥ 0, (1.6) 

∂τ un = ν∆un − ∇ pn−1 − gn−1 for x ∈ Ω and n ≥ 1, (1.7) subjected to the boundary condition un = 0 on ∂Ω. The stability result of the semi-discrete first-order consistent splitting scheme for the time dependent Stokes equations with no-slip boundary conditions (resp., with periodic-nonperiodic boundary conditions in a periodic channel) was estab-lished in [9], and the local-in-time stability and error estimates for INSE were proven in a series of work by Liu, Liu and Pego [19, 20]. By using the second order backward differentiation for-mula D2vn := 32 ∂τ vn − 12 ∂τ vn−1 and the second-order explicit extrapolation ˆvn := 2 vn−1 − vn−2, a second-order consistent splitting scheme [9, 14] was formulated as follows, 

⟨∇ pn, ∇ϕ⟩ = ⟨ν∆un − ν∇∇ · un − gn, ∇ϕ⟩ for ∀ϕ ∈ H1(Ω) and n ≥ 0,

D2un = ν∆un − ∇ ˆpn − ˆgn for x ∈ Ω with un = 0 on ∂Ω and n ≥ 2.However, whether the second-order consistent splitting scheme is stable remains open since its inception. Actually, as pointed out by [10, 12], it has been a long standing open question on how to 2construct unconditionally stable second- or higher-order decoupled scheme with a rigorous stability and error analysis. The situation was essentially changed after the series of works [10–12] by Huang and Shen, in which a class of k-th ( 2 ≤ k ≤ 5) order generalized implicit-explicit backward differentiation formulas (GBDF-k) with a free parameter β (β ≥ 1) was designed and applied to develop some high-order unconditionally stable schemes. They proved in [11, Theorem 2] that the GBDF2 and GBDF3 methods for β > 1, and the GBDF4 method for β ≥ 2 are stable for linear parabolic problems; while [11, Theorem 3] establishes the corresponding convergence for a class of nonlinear parabolic problems by assuming that the nonlinear term is locally Lipschitz continuous. By choosing three fixed parameters βk = 3 , 6, 9 corresponding to the order indexes k = 2 , 3, 4, respectively, Huang and Shen [12] established the unconditional stability and convergence of GBDF-k consistent splitting schemes in the ℓ∞(H1) ∩ ℓ2(H2) norm for the INSE. Note that, the theoretical analysis in [11, 12] always takes advantage of the Dahlquist’s G-stability theory, a new convolution-type multiplier and certain decomposition of implicit part, cf. [11, (3.5)] and [12, (3.16)]. The significant developments in [10–12] raise some new questions: why can the GBDF-k con-sistent splitting methods achieve the unconditional stability for the INSE, or why choose the free parameters βk = 3 , 6, 9 for the order indexes k = 2 , 3, 4, respectively? What kind of implicit-explicit linear multistep (IELM) methods can also make the associated consistent splitting algorithms un-conditionally stable for the INSE? This article answers these questions by presenting a concise, unified theory on the unconditional stability and convergence of general IELM methods for the unconstrained INSE system (1.4)-(1.5): • With the help of the Stokes pressure estimate from [19, Theorem 1.2], we apply the recent semi-generating function approach [16] and the global discrete energy analysis to establish the unconditional stability of consistent splitting IELM methods with respect to the ℓ∞(H1) ∩

ℓ2(H2) norm if the associated implicit-explicit controllability intensity I(k) IE > √2/2.• By choosing proper large parameters, the recently proposed β-parameterized GBDF-k (for 

2 ≤ k ≤ 5) schemes in [11, 12] and γ-parameterized SIELM-k (for 2 ≤ k ≤ 9) schemes in [16] can fulfill I(k) IE > √2/2 so that they can theoretically achieve the unconditional stability of the associated consistent splitting IELM methods. They present a positive answer to the first open issue proposed by Huang and Shen [12]. • Extensive experiments are presented to examine the stability and convergence of GBDF-k and SIELM-k methods for different parameters. They suggest that our theoretical requirement 

I(k) IE > √2/2 is sufficient but always not sharp. Note that, our theory would be theoretically concise since the stability and convergence analysis do not involve any Nevanlinna-Odeh type multipliers for high-order implicit-explicit BDF methods [1–4] or any delicate decompositions of implicit part for the GBDF-k methods [11, 12].
